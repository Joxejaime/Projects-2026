# Game day log

> **These are exercises on a local lab service, not production incidents.** Numbers are from single runs against a
> demo service on one machine; they show the *method* and how the tooling behaves, not production performance.
> Per-run reports (generated by `chaos/runner.py`): [checkout-errors](checkout-errors.md), [checkout-latency](checkout-latency.md).

## 2026-10-25: first two experiments

| Experiment | Injected | Client-side during fault | Server-side SLI (own `/metrics`) | Control route (`/catalog`) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| checkout-error-injection | 30% HTTP 500 on `/checkout` for 20 s | 30.3% errors | 30.2% bad | 0.0% errors | held |
| checkout-latency-injection | +400 ms on `/checkout` for 20 s | 0.0% errors, 99.8% slower than 300 ms (p95 459 ms) | 99.8% bad (latency SLI) | 0.0% errors | held |

What the pair demonstrates:
- **The two SLIs are independent.** The latency fault produced zero errors, so the availability SLO would have stayed green
  while the latency SLO burned at 100x. Alerting on only one of them would have missed it.
- **Client and server agree.** The server-side SLI computed from the service's own counters matched what the runner
  measured, which is the cross-check that the SLI definitions measure the real thing.
- **Faults are contained per route.** The control route stayed clean in both runs.
